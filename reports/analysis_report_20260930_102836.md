## 5. Hotspot Analysis & Explainable Priority-Zone Classification

The Amazon pilot study area was tessellated into square geographic cells of **0.25° × 0.25°** (approximately 27.7 km per side). Every cell aggregates thermal detections and derives an **explainable 0–100 score**.

### Priority Score Formula & Weights Applied
The composite score evaluates four key dimensions of fire activity:

$$\text{Priority Score} = \left( w_{\text{freq}} \cdot \hat{F} + w_{\text{rec}} \cdot \hat{R} + w_{\text{frp}} \cdot \hat{I} + w_{\text{conf}} \cdot \hat{C} \right) \times 100$$

| Factor | Active Weight | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Detection Frequency ($\hat{F}$)** | **45.0%** | Min-max scaled total detection volume |
| **Recent Activity ($\hat{R}$)** | **25.0%** | Detections recorded in the latest 48-hour window |
| **Mean Intensity / FRP ($\hat{I}$)** | **20.0%** | Normalized average Fire Radiative Power (MW) |
| **Confidence Level ($\hat{C}$)** | **10.0%** | Proportion of high/nominal confidence satellite detections |

### Priority Thresholds
- **High Priority** (Score $\ge 70.0$): Persistent, high-volume, or severe thermal clusters requiring immediate attention.
- **Medium Priority** (Score $35.0 - 69.9$): Moderate activity or emergent clusters.
- **Low Priority** (Score $< 35.0$): Low detection density or isolated thermal anomalies.

## 7. Data Quality & Limitations Notice

> **CRITICAL SCIENTIFIC & OPERATIONAL LIMITATIONS:**  
> NASA FIRMS records active-fire detections or thermal anomalies. A detection alone does not prove a confirmed wildfire. The Fire Activity Priority Score is an analytical ranking based on stored detections, not a wildfire prediction or official emergency warning.

> A thermal anomaly indicates a pixel with significant temperature contrast compared to its ambient background. > Detections can stem from agricultural burns, industrial flaring, hot ground features, or atmospheric reflection. > Prioritization scores represent relative statistical activity inside the current Firestore dataset window and > must not be cited as predictive wildfire occurrence forecasts.
